# Anatomy examination preparation

For every structure, ask where it is, what it connects with, what it does and which pathway supplies it. Distinguish what a question actually asks: motor nerve, general sensation, taste, artery, lymph route, movement or tissue type. The same region can have different answers for each category.

Use the atlas actively. Identify the view and side, locate landmarks, look away and reconstruct the relationship in words. Then check the linked concepts and tables. Read clarification notes when a source image uses a simplified statement. An original label remains visible in the atlas even when the learning note gives a more precise account.

High-value contrasts include concha/meatus/ostium/sinus; anterior/posterior bleeding; artery/nerve/lymph; inferior/superior TMJ spaces; condyle/coronoid; V3 mastication versus XII tongue movement; tongue touch versus taste; IX exceptions; pharyngeal regions; C6/T10/T11 boundaries; and nasal escape versus penetration versus aspiration.

During instant-feedback practice, explain the selected key and why alternatives do not fit. Do not memorize a display letter because option order changes. Revisit missed questions later without relying on the earlier order. Definition and misconception items support foundations; mock exams select from the 120 focused cases.

Try 30-, 60- or 120-question mock exams after the modules. They sample equal numbers from six modules, not a supplied university blueprint. Review by module and return to the relevant lesson. Repeated cases can improve recall without proving performance on unseen questions. Written-answer models demonstrate a defensible response, not an official marking scheme.

For clinical cases, separate mechanism from diagnosis. A regional relationship can suggest a possibility but does not establish a patient’s condition. A gag reflex does not guarantee swallowing safety; blood exit route does not identify an exact artery; hoarseness does not establish malignancy; and one muscle action does not explain every coordinated task.
